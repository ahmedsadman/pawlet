Pawlet Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 October 2026 · Applies to the Pawlet Android app (com.pastabyte.pawlet)

In short: Pawlet has no user accounts and no third-party analytics. It reads your bank SMS messages on your device to build a personal finance ledger, and that ledger is stored only on your phone. The one time message content leaves your device is when an unrecognised message has to be classified by an AI model. That text is handled in transit and is never written to a database or a log file by us. We keep no copy of your messages, transactions, or balances — only an anonymous installation identifier and a few counts about it: when it was seen and active, its app version and device integrity verdict, how many classification requests it made, and how many messages the on-device model handled by itself versus sent to the cloud model. Those counts are what prevent the service being abused and tell us how well the on-device model works.

1. Who we are and what this covers

This is the privacy policy for Pawlet, an Android application published on Google Play under the package name com.pastabyte.pawlet. Pawlet is developed and published by PastaByte ("we", "us", "the developer"). This policy explains how the app accesses, collects, uses, shares, retains, and deletes your information, and it applies to all versions of the app distributed on Google Play.

2. Information Pawlet handles

SMS messages

Pawlet's core function is reading transaction notifications sent by your bank or card issuer over SMS. With your permission, the app reads incoming messages and your existing SMS inbox. For each message it keeps the sender, the message text, and the timestamp on your device.

Messages that are not financial are discarded; records of ignored messages are automatically deleted after 7 days.

Financial information derived from those messages

From the messages, Pawlet extracts and stores transaction amounts, dates, merchant or description text, bank and card names, and bill due dates. You may also enter this information manually. This data is used only to display your balances, spending, and bills inside the app.

Installation identifier and usage counts

When Pawlet uses our classification service (see section 4), the app generates a random installation identifier. It is not your name, your phone number, your Google account, or any hardware identifier, and it is created by the app itself. Before it is stored, it is irreversibly hashed. Against that hash we record only:

  • the dates the installation was first and last seen, and the days on which it was active;
  • the number of classification requests it made each day, and the number of AI tokens those requests used;
  • its app version, the device integrity tier and Play licensing verdict reported by Google Play Integrity, and the Android SDK level;
  • for each day, how many messages the on-device model classified by itself, how many it passed on to the cloud model, and how many it could not process.

None of this includes message content, senders, categories, amounts, balances, or anything else about your finances.

This exists for two reasons: to enforce fair-use quotas and block abuse of a service we pay for, and to measure how well the on-device model works, so that fewer messages need to leave your phone. It is never linked to your messages or your financial data, because we do not retain those. A fresh identifier is generated if you reinstall the app, and we have no way to connect a new one to an old one.

What Pawlet does not collect

  • No name, email address, phone number, or date of birth.
  • No user account — there is nothing to sign up for or log in to.
  • No advertising identifier, and no hardware or operating system device identifier such as IMEI, IMSI, SIM serial number, MAC address, or Android ID.
  • No location data.
  • No third-party analytics, crash-reporting, or advertising SDKs of any kind. The only usage data Pawlet sends is the anonymous, aggregate counts described above.
  • No contacts, photos, files, camera, or microphone access.

3. Where your data is stored

Your messages, transactions, bills, and bank details are stored on your device only, in the app's private storage area that other apps cannot read. Specifically:

  • Your ledger lives in a local database inside Pawlet's private app sandbox. On modern Android devices this storage is covered by the operating system's own device encryption.
  • If you supply your own AI provider API key, it is stored in the Android Keystore, which is hardware-backed encrypted storage.
  • Your app lock PIN is stored locally and is never transmitted.

We do not operate any cloud sync, cloud backup, or remote copy of your ledger. The only data held on our servers is the installation hash and the counts described in section 2.

4. When data leaves your device

AI classification

Pawlet classifies messages in three stages. The first two run entirely on your device: a sender and keyword filter, then a bundled on-device machine-learning model. Only when those stages cannot confidently classify a message does Pawlet fall back to a cloud AI model.

In that fallback case, the message sender ID, the message text, and your selected currency code are transmitted over an encrypted HTTPS connection so the model can determine whether the message is financial and extract the amount and merchant.

Bank messages may contain details such as a masked card number or an account balance, so you should be aware of what your bank sends you. No name, email, phone number, or device identifier is attached to the request, and Pawlet holds no account that it could be linked to.

Depending on your version of the app and whether you have supplied your own API key, that request takes one of two routes:

  • Directly from your device to OpenRouter, an AI model routing provider. We are not involved in the request and never see it.
  • Through Pawlet's classification service, which forwards the request to OpenRouter on your behalf using our API key. In this case the message text passes through our server in memory only. It is never written to a database, never written to a log file, and never retained after the response is returned. Our request logs record only the HTTP method, path, response status, and duration — never the body.

In both routes, the message text reaches OpenRouter and the model provider it selects, and is handled there under OpenRouter's privacy policy.

If you prefer that nothing leaves your device at all, you can decline network access. The on-device stages continue to work offline, and messages the on-device model cannot classify are simply left unprocessed.

Abuse prevention and app integrity

Before the classification service will accept requests, the app proves it is a genuine, unmodified copy of Pawlet using Google Play Integrity. The app obtains a token from Google Play services and sends it to our server, which asks Google to decode it. Google returns only verdicts — whether the app binary is genuine, whether the device passes basic integrity checks, and whether the install came from Play. Google does not disclose your Google account, email, or device identity to us through this process, and we never see it.

On success the server issues a session token valid for 24 hours. It contains only the installation hash and a random value, and is not stored by us.

Your IP address is visible to our server when your device connects, as it is to any internet service. We use it transiently to rate-limit requests and discard it within the hour. IP addresses are never written to disk and are never associated with your installation identifier or with any message content.

Daily message counts

If you use Pawlet's classification service, the app also sends our server, at most a few times a day, a short summary covering today and the previous 30 days: for each day and app version, how many messages the on-device model classified by itself, how many it passed on to the cloud model, and how many it could not process. The summary travels over HTTPS with the same session token as classification requests, so it is stored against the installation hash described in section 2. It contains no message text, sender, category, amount, or balance. Messages imported from your existing inbox are not counted. If you use your own API key, or no AI service at all, the app never sends it.

Currency exchange rates

Pawlet fetches public currency exchange rates from open.er-api.com. This is an anonymous request for public rate data. No personal, financial, or message data is included.

5. Backup and restore

Pawlet lets you export your data to a backup file. The file is created on your device and handed to Android's share sheet so that you choose where it goes — for example your own cloud drive or messaging app. The temporary copy is deleted after sharing. We never receive it.

The backup contains your messages, transactions, bills, banks, and settings. It does not contain your app lock PIN or your API key. Because the backup file is not encrypted, store it somewhere you control.

8. Retention and deletion

On your device

  • Your ledger is retained on your device for as long as you choose to keep it. We apply no retention period of our own.
  • Records of messages identified as non-financial are automatically and permanently deleted after 7 days.
  • You can delete individual transactions, bills, and message records at any time inside the app.
  • Clearing the app's storage in Android Settings, or uninstalling Pawlet, permanently and irreversibly deletes all of its data from your device.

On our servers

  • Message text and classification results: not retained at all. They exist only in memory for the duration of the request.
  • Installation hash, request counts, active days, and app and device details: retained indefinitely, because historical request volume is what lets us detect and block abuse. This data cannot be used to identify you or to reconstruct anything about your finances.
  • Daily on-device message counts: kept against the installation hash for 90 days. After that they are added into daily totals that carry no installation hash, and the installation keeps only a single running total of the messages counted.
  • Session tokens expire 24 hours after issue and are not stored.
  • Attestation challenges are held in memory for 2 minutes and then discarded.
  • IP addresses used for rate limiting are held in memory and discarded within the hour. They are never written to disk.

Because the installation hash is anonymous by design, neither you nor we can trace a stored record back to a particular person or device, so we are unable to locate and delete an individual record on request. Uninstalling the app permanently severs any connection between you and that record: the identifier is generated fresh on reinstall, and we have no means of linking a new identifier to an old one. The record that remains holds nothing but a hash and the counts described in section 2.

Data sent for AI classification

Message text sent to OpenRouter is retained and deleted according to OpenRouter's own privacy policy and that of the model provider handling the request. We have no control over, and no access to, that data once the request is made.

Accounts

Pawlet does not offer account creation. There is no sign-up, no login, and no user account associated with the app, and therefore no account for you to delete and no account data held by us.

9. Security

Pawlet stores data in Android's private per-app storage, keeps credentials in the hardware-backed Keystore, offers an optional PIN and biometric app lock, and uses HTTPS for every network request. On the server side, installation identifiers are irreversibly hashed before storage, message content is never persisted or logged, session tokens are cryptographically signed and short-lived, and access is gated behind Google Play Integrity attestation.

No system is perfectly secure, and the security of your device — its screen lock, operating system updates, and where you store exported backups — remains an important part of protecting your information.

10. Children

Pawlet is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
